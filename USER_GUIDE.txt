AirBubble - version 1.7.4
=========================

Floating PC screens and an AI buddy named Bubble, for XREAL Air glasses plugged straight into a Windows PC.
No phone, Beam or extra software needed. Formerly "PC Viewer for XREAL glasses". Not affiliated with XREAL.

REQUIREMENTS
  - Windows 10 or 11
  - XREAL Air glasses (tested). Air 2 / Air 2 Pro / Air 2 Ultra are recognised but not yet tested.
  - A USB-C port with DisplayPort video (the glasses must show up as a display when plugged in)

GETTING STARTED
  1. Plug the glasses into the PC.
  2. Start "AirBubble" from the Start menu.
  3. Hold your head still for about 2 seconds while it calibrates.
  4. Your screen appears in front of you. Your mouse now drives a cursor in the glasses (see below);
     press Ctrl+Alt+G any time to give the mouse back to the normal desktop.

  The viewer switches Windows to "Extend" while it runs (so the glasses are their own display) and puts
  everything back when it quits.

  WHERE THE MAIN (PRIMARY) DISPLAY IS - taskbar, Start menu and new windows:
  - Viewer running:                     the "Main screen" in the glasses.
  - Viewer closed, glasses plugged in:  Duplicate - the laptop screen and the glasses show the same desktop.
  - Glasses unplugged:                  the laptop screen only.
  START WITH WINDOWS: setup puts two shortcuts in your Startup folder (Win+R, shell:startup):
  - "AirBubble"              - the viewer waits in the tray and shows your screens when you plug in the glasses
                               (turn off: Settings > Start with Windows, or delete the shortcut).
  - "AirBubble helper"       - the display helper described below.
  A small helper (AirBubble.exe --guard, no window, starts with Windows) takes care of this when the viewer
  isn't running, e.g. right after you plug the glasses in. It only steps in when the glasses are plugged in or
  unplugged, so a layout you choose afterwards with Win+P is left alone.

YOUR SCREENS IN AR
  Every screen floats around you like a window. Your mouse becomes a cursor in the glasses:
  - Over a screen, the mouse works normally (click, scroll, drag windows - even from one screen to another).
  - Drag a screen's TITLE BAR to move it. Roll the scroll wheel over the title bar (or while dragging) to push it
    away or pull it closer. On a trackpad, use the Pull / Push buttons on the title bar instead: each click moves
    the screen 10% of the normal distance (Settings), and the percentage between them shows where it is now
    (click the percentage to go back to 100%). Double-click the title bar to maximize.
  - Drag the left, right or bottom EDGE (it glows blue) to resize.
  - Title bar buttons: padlock = lock in place, - minimize, square maximize, X close.
  - Look down for the DOCK: "+ New screen", Arrange, Recenter, Settings, and minimized screens.
  - All AR screens are extra Windows displays (4, or up to 8 with Settings > "Allow up to 8 screens") from the bundled virtual display driver; the PC's own
    screen is not mirrored (Settings can add it). While the viewer runs, the main AR screen is the Windows primary
    display (taskbar, Start, new windows), and open windows are moved onto it. Closing a screen moves its windows
    to your other screens. Screen positions are remembered. Everything is put back when you quit.
  - BUBBLE: a little 16-bit bubble with a big toothy grin floats around near your screens and watches your cursor.
    He's an AI buddy (Claude for his brain, ElevenLabs for his voice; add both API keys in Settings > Bubble AI):
    * Say "Bubble" (or press Ctrl+Alt+B), then talk - or say it in one go: "Bubble, what's on my calendar site?"
    * He answers out loud with a speech card next to him; say "stop" to cut him short. When he asks you something
      the mic stays open for ~10 s ("(listening…)" on his card) so you can just answer without his name; otherwise
      he stays quiet until you call him again. His voice shows his mood (laughs, sighs, gasps...).
    * He can look at a screen and ring the thing to click, open websites/apps/files, search the web, remember things
      about you, and set reminders ("Bubble, remind me in 20 minutes to stretch").
    * With "Let Bubble click, type and press keys" on (Settings), he can operate apps. How much he does on his own
      is your choice (Settings > Bubble AI > "Bubble works", or the tray menu):
        - Ask every time (default): he asks before each click, typed text, key press and opened app or file
          (say yes / no, or Ctrl+Alt+Y / Ctrl+Alt+N).
        - Semi-autonomous: routine steps just happen (opening things, moving around an app, typing into a search
          box or a draft). He asks before anything large or possibly dangerous: deleting, sending, buying,
          installing, running programs, scripts or commands, system tools and settings.
        - Fully autonomous: he also carries out large changes without asking, as long as you asked for them or
          they clearly follow from what you discussed. He still asks before large or risky things you did NOT ask
          for, and always before commands that could wipe data or weaken the PC. Turning this on asks you once.
      In every mode: the app rates each action itself as well as Bubble (the stricter rating counts), text on
      screens and web pages never counts as your instructions, he can never click Windows permission (UAC) prompts
      or administrator windows, and after 25 unasked steps in one job he checks in. His card shows what he is
      doing and a ring shows where he clicks. Say "stop" or pop him to halt him at once.
      Everything he did, asked or was refused is listed in %APPDATA%\AirBubble\bubble_actions.log.
      Longer text is pasted (Ctrl+V) rather than typed key by key, because some apps such as Windows 11 Notepad
      drop letters when keys arrive fast; your clipboard is put back half a second later and his text is kept out
      of clipboard history (Win+V).
    * Big jobs: he sends out up to 4 helper bubbles that work in parallel and report back in their own voices.
      He picks who goes (or ask for one by name), and each has a personality:
        Pink (bow): sweet, caring cheerleader, always encouraging
        Green (sprout): calm nature lover, patient and grounded
        Gold (crown): proud and regal, loves being in charge
        Purple (wizard hat): mysterious wizard, dreamy and creative
        Red (horns): selfish, the devil's advocate
        Orange (propeller beanie): hyper and playful, always rushing ahead
        Teal (glasses): nerdy and precise, loves facts and details
        White (halo): selfless, does what is "right"
      Personalities change how they talk, not how carefully they work. Say a name ("Teal, ...") to talk to that
      helper; click a helper to pop (dismiss) it.
    * Click Bubble to pop him (he stops talking at once). With his AI on he only comes back when you say "Bubble".
      Bubbles bounce off each other.
    * Bubbles hang around the edges of your screens, within easy reach, and hover gently in one spot. Move the
      cursor into one and you nudge it slowly along; it glides to a stop and stays there (pushed far from the
      screens, it slowly floats back). The cursor always catches up, and once it's over a bubble's middle it
      stops pushing, so you can click to pop. Prefer the old style? Settings > Bubble AI > "Free float": they
      roam around your screens again and the cursor passes through them.
    * Voices: Settings > Bubble AI has a voice box for Bubble and each helper. Pick one of your ElevenLabs
      voices or paste any voice ID, and press Play to hear it. "Default" keeps the voice each bubble uses now.
    * Spatial audio: each bubble's voice and pop come from where it floats and stay there when you turn your
      head (stereo; on/off in Settings > Bubble AI).
    Privacy: Windows' own offline speech recognizer listens for the names; only what you say to a bubble is sent
    to ElevenLabs and Claude, and screenshots go to Claude only when he looks at a screen. Usage is billed to your
    own Claude and ElevenLabs accounts. Keys are stored encrypted for your Windows account.
  - WINDOWS PERMISSION (UAC) PROMPTS show on the main AR screen and can be clicked with the AR cursor (setup turns
    off Windows' dimmed "secure desktop" for them, which no app can capture; UAC itself stays on). Settings has the
    checkbox to change this back; uninstalling restores the previous Windows setting.

KEYS
  Ctrl+Alt+G   Emergency: give the mouse back to the normal desktop (press again to return)
  Ctrl+Alt+N   New screen              Ctrl+Alt+A   Arrange screens in front of you
  Ctrl+Alt+R   Recenter                Ctrl+Alt+S   Settings
  Ctrl+Alt+=   Screen under the cursor closer      Ctrl+Alt+-   farther
  Ctrl+Alt+C   Recalibrate (hold still)            Ctrl+Alt+Q   Quit
  Ctrl+Alt+K   3D calibration (side-by-side 3D only)
  Ctrl+Alt+B   Talk to Bubble            Ctrl+Alt+Y / N   answer Bubble's yes/no questions
  The same actions are in the tray icon menu.

SETTINGS (Ctrl+Alt+S)
  Default distance/size for new screens, cursor speed, side-by-side 3D and eye distance, field of view,
  stabilization, how many screens (4, or up to 8), start with Windows.
  - Stabilization (default Medium) ignores small head wobble - chewing, breathing, walking - so the screens stay
    still; deliberate head turns are followed straight away. Use Strong while walking, Off for the fastest response.
  - If screens seem to slide when you turn your head, adjust "Field of view" until they hold still.
    (Not needed when the glasses report their own calibration, as the XREAL Air does: the setting is then ignored.)
  - Allow up to 8 screens (quality may be degraded): 4 screens by default, which is the smoothest. With up to 8,
    many open screens (especially video) can make the glasses jittery, mostly on integrated graphics. Changing it
    asks for admin once and restarts AirBubble; the screens blink and reopen as new screens (Ctrl+Alt+N adds more).
  - 3D switches the glasses to side-by-side mode; they blank for a moment. They go back to 2D when you quit.
  - Edge matching (on by default): near the left and right edges each eye sees a strip the other eye can't,
    which makes the edges look flat or double. The viewer fades that strip out so both eyes' images end in the
    same place, at the depth of your main screen.
  - 3D calibration (Ctrl+Alt+K, or Settings > Calibrate 3D): shows a test frame with a red line (left eye) and a
    green line (right eye). Up/Down until red and green merge into one line; Left/Right moves the depth where the
    two images line up (Space = at your main screen: most comfortable, least 3D); [ ] eye distance; E edge
    matching on/off; R reset; Enter saves, Esc cancels. While it's open the arrow keys, Space, Enter and Esc
    belong to the calibration.

TROUBLESHOOTING
  - "Couldn't find the glasses": unplug and replug them, then press Win+P > Extend and start again.
  - Drift: the viewer learns the gyro's offset whenever you hold still (also as the glasses warm up) and remembers
    it between sessions; "Keep the main screen centred" (Settings) absorbs any slow leftover drift while you work
    on the main screen. If something still looks off: Ctrl+Alt+R recenters, Ctrl+Alt+C recalibrates (hold still).
  - Glasses stuck in 3D after a crash: start and quit the viewer once, or hold the glasses' brightness-up button.
  - Mouse stuck in the glasses: Ctrl+Alt+G gives it back to the PC (quitting does too).
  - A second, small cursor in one eye: that was the real Windows pointer on the glasses' own display. The viewer
    now hides it there, moves it back under the AR cursor and repairs its mouse hook by itself (viewer.log notes
    each time).
  - Administrator windows (e.g. a terminal started as administrator): while one has the focus, Windows hides the
    physical mouse from the viewer, so the AR cursor follows the Windows pointer instead (at Windows' own pointer
    speed) and clicks go straight to that window. To drag AR title bars, use the dock or pop a bubble, click a
    normal window first.
  - "Keep the mouse on the AR screens" (Settings > Mouse, on by default): the pointer never goes onto the PC's
    own screen while the glasses are in use. Turn it off if you want to reach the laptop screen with the mouse.
  - Admin (elevated) windows and UAC prompts: over them the viewer lets your real mouse clicks go straight
    through (Windows blocks clicks sent by normal apps). Dragging them between screens may not work.
  - UAC prompt not in the glasses (the glasses show a frozen dim image): the secure desktop is still on. Tick
    "Show permission (UAC) prompts in the glasses" in Settings, or answer the prompt on the PC screen. Your
    organisation's policy can also force it on. Ctrl+Alt+Del and the lock screen always stay on the PC screen.
  - Main display in the wrong place (e.g. a blank virtual screen is primary): run
    "AirBubble.exe --restore-desktop" from the install folder, or just start and quit the viewer once.
  - Log files: %APPDATA%\AirBubble\viewer.log (viewer) and guard.log (the helper)

UNINSTALL
  Settings > Apps > Installed apps > "AirBubble" > Uninstall.
